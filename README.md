# ADR Identification Basics - Task 1

## Patient Case Scenario: Suspected ACE-Inhibitor Induced Cough

### Patient Profile & Medical History
* **Patient:** 58-year-old female
* **Past Medical History:** Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (diagnosed 5 years ago), Essential Hypertension (diagnosed 3 years ago).
* **Social History:** Non-smoker, occasional alcohol use. No known drug allergies.

### Current Chronic Medications
* Metformin 1000 mg twice daily (stable for 4 years)
* Amlodipine 5 mg once daily (stable for 3 years)

### Recent Clinical Timeline & Symptoms
* **Day 1:** The patient presents to the clinic with elevated blood pressure (150/95 mmHg). The physician prescribes a new medication, **Lisinopril 10 mg once daily**, to optimize blood pressure control.
* **Day 14:** The patient develops a persistent, dry, hacking cough.
* **Day 21:** The patient returns to the clinic reporting that the cough is interfering with her sleep. She reports no fever, no shortness of breath, no nasal congestion, and no recent exposure to sick individuals. 

## ADR Identification & Causality Analysis

1. **Review of Symptoms:** The patient presents with a dry, non-productive cough without systemic signs of infection (no fever, no purulent sputum), ruling out common respiratory infections like bronchitis or pneumonia.
2. **Medication History Evaluation:** Metformin and Amlodipine have been taken consistently for years without adverse effects, making them highly unlikely culprits for a newly emergent symptom. 
3. **Timeline & Plausibility:** The onset of the cough occurred exactly two weeks after initiating Lisinopril. An accumulation of bradykinin and substance P in the respiratory tract is a well-documented pharmacological mechanism of Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitors like Lisinopril, typically presenting within 1 to 4 weeks of therapy initiation.
4. **Suspected Drug:** Lisinopril.

## Proposed Action & Dechallenge
To confirm the Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR), the suspected drug (Lisinopril) should be discontinued (positive dechallenge) and substituted with an Angiotensin II Receptor Blocker (ARB), such as Losartan, which does not interfere with bradykinin breakdown. If the cough resolves within 1 to 4 weeks of discontinuation, the ADR causality can be classified as "Probable" according to the Naranjo Causality Scale.
